\section{Literature Review}

A substantial body of international literature associates exposure to
childhood violence with poorer educational outcomes. The systematic review
and meta-analysis conducted by \citet{FRY20186} examined evidence from 21
countries and found that childhood violence was associated with lower
academic achievement, greater absenteeism and an increased likelihood of
school dropout.

More specific evidence on peer bullying is provided by
\citet{ozyildirim2024effect}, who conducted a meta-analysis using TIMSS and PIRLS
results from 77 countries. Although the estimated associations were
relatively small, bullying had its largest association with reading
achievement when compared with mathematics and science. The association
was also significantly larger among Grade~4 learners than among Grade~8
learners. These findings make the PIRLS Grade~4 sample particularly
appropriate for examining the relationship between bullying frequency and
reading achievement. Bullying is particularly
prevalent in South African schools: approximately 73--74\% of learners in
PIRLS~2021 reported experiencing bullying about monthly or about weekly
\citep{mullis2023pirls}. Historical evidence also indicates that bullying is
associated with poorer educational achievement. Using PIRLS, prePIRLS and
TIMSS~2011 data from Botswana, Ghana and South Africa,
\citet{anton_erxleben2016bullying} found substantial achievement differences between bullied and
non-bullied learners after matching them on observed background
characteristics.

Exposure to bullying is nevertheless not distributed evenly among South
African learners. Using nationally representative TIMSS~2019 data,
\citet{juan2025risk} found that age, gender and socioeconomic status were
associated with the likelihood of experiencing different forms of
victimisation. Boys and older learners generally faced a greater risk,
whereas higher socioeconomic status provided some protection against most
forms of bullying.

Brazil provides a useful comparison with South Africa because the two countries share several economic and educational characteristics. Approximately 42\% of Brazilian learners reported experiencing bullying monthly or weekly in PIRLS 2021, considerably below the corresponding proportion in South Africa \citep{mullis2023pirls}. Regardless, both countries are upper-middle-income members of BRICS, that have highly unequal income distributions and have implemented comparable education policies \citep{de2023comparative,spaull2023pirls}. Nevertheless, Brazil performs approximately three years ahead of South Africa in reading achievement. This contrast provides a basis for examining whether the association between bullying and reading performance differs between two unequal emerging economies with comparable policy contexts. Evidence from Brazil also supports the approach used in this essay. Using PISA 2015 data, \citet{sbrogliorizzotto2021bullying} found that the relationship between bullying and school performance differed according to both the frequency and type of bullying experienced. Physical bullying and being threatened were associated with lower reading performance, while other forms showed weaker, insignificant or different results. These findings support the use of frequency categories in this essay, as learners who experience bullying weekly may be affected differently from those who experience it monthly or hardly ever.

Although Ireland is a substantially different context from South Africa, the longitudinal cohort study by \citet{murphy2022educational} provides useful evidence on the relationship between bullying and achievement. The findings suggest that bullying at age nine predicted later psychological difficulties, which were associated with lower literacy and numeracy scores. This supports the proposed mechanism in which bullying affects achievement partly through learners' psychological difficulties. Psychological difficulties are therefore not included as a control in the main model, as doing so could remove part of the relationship this essay aims to estimate.
