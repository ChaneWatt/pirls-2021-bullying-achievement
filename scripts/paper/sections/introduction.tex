\section{Introduction}

The most prominent headline within the South African education sphere is that 81\% of grade 4 learners can not read for meaning. Many studies have attempted to find the underlying mechanisms that resulted in these low test scores with the natural inclination coming to mind being learners social economic status, the school quantile or the confidence of the learners. In 2016 however \citet{anton_erxleben2016bullying} found that bullying is one of the key drivers of lower  academic performance illustrated in their Directed Acyclic Graph results. This paper will be exploring whether bullying is associated with poorer reading achievement. this will then look at whether some learners are affected more than others, specifically by gender, school quintiles, different Social Economics Status. 

On a global level SOuth Africa is performing the lowest of the 57 countries that participated in the PIRLS assignment.


Is bullying associated with poorer reading achievement among South African learners, does this association differ by learner characteristics, and how does the South African pattern compare with Brazil?

Is bullying associated with lower reading achievement in South Africa?
Does the association differ by gender, socioeconomic status, or age/over-age status?
Is the magnitude and pattern of the association similar in Brazil?
